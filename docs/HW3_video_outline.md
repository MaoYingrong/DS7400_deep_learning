# HW3 video outline (3 minutes max)

| Time | Show on screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | README, HW2 results table | My HW2 model predicts a physics paper's field from its title, its citation neighbours' titles and its journal. It reaches 77% test accuracy but overfits: 93% train vs 75% validation, and almost all 161K parameters are in the first layer. |
| 0:25–1:05 | `results/hw3/architecture.png`, then `training/hw3_model.py` forward() | The first layer learns separate weights for the paper's own title and its neighbours' titles, although both use the same vocabulary. My design: one shared encoder for both (half the parameters), and a learned per-paper gate that decides how much neighbour information to add, like a residual correction. I expected less overfitting, better test accuracy, and a gate that closes for papers with unhelpful neighbours. Risks: the two inputs may need different weights, and the gate may saturate. |
| 1:05–1:35 | Terminal: `python training/hw3_experiments.py` output; `hw3_config.json` | Same data, split, optimiser and 40 epochs for everything; 10 seeds each. Besides my design I ran ablations: no gate, untied weights, one global gate, no neighbours, plus an 80-epoch check. |
| 1:35–2:30 | Results table, `curves.png`, `gates.png` | Tying cut the train–validation gap from 18 to 13.5 points and raised validation accuracy, but test accuracy dropped 0.9 points, about 3 papers, so really it is the same accuracy with half the parameters. The gate failed: it opened to 0.97 for every paper regardless of neighbour count, so the gated and ungated models are identical. Neighbours are worth 5 points, so the loss always pushes the gate open. Surprise: the single global gate was worst, because Adam could only move its one parameter from 0.5 to 0.68 in 40 epochs. |
| 2:30–3:00 | Report section 5 | What I learned: a gate only helps when the loss rewards different values for different examples; otherwise it collapses to a constant. And on 300 test papers a one-point change is inside the noise. Next: several random splits, feed the neighbour count to the gate and let it exceed 1, and look at high-degree papers, where removing neighbours seemed to help. |
